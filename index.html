Are Central Asian cities heating up faster than their surroundings?

Surface urban heat islands · 30 cities · summers 2003–2025

The whole region is warming, so the countryside heats up too. Subtracting it from the city leaves what the city itself adds: buildings, asphalt, lost greenery. That gap is the surface urban heat island, and this map shows whether it is growing.

Reading the colours. Red: the city is hotter than its surroundings (or the gap is growing). Blue: cooler (or the gap is shrinking). By day, desert and semi-desert cities are often cool islands: irrigation, trees and shade versus scorching bare ground. At night it flips almost everywhere, as concrete and asphalt release the heat stored during the day.

Data. Land surface temperature (LST) from Aqua MODIS MYD11A2 v6.1, 1 km, June–August, ~13:30 and ~01:30 local time. City = the contiguous GHSL 2020 urban centre (class 30) around the city point; surroundings = GHSL rural cells (classes 11–13) within 50 km and within ±100 m of the city’s elevation. Trend = Sen’s slope, significance = Mann–Kendall test.

Caveats. LST is the temperature of the ground and roofs, not the air. The gap also moves when the surroundings change: ploughing, irrigation or desertification (e.g. around Nukus and Kyzylorda as the Aral Sea dried up).
